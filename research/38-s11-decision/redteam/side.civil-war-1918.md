**Skew and potential tool bias**
The set flags Side A **5.5×** more than Side B. Partly this could reflect **editor traffic**: Finnish Wikipedia follows modern *sisällissota* ("civil war") historiography, so White-revisionist pushes clash with baseline text more often, and unclustered repeat edits (#2/#14 on Lenin; #11/#12 on Ostrobothnia; #7/#9/#15 on "legitimate/official" government) inflate A's count.

However, **tool bias remains plausible**:
1. **Consensus-anchoring bias:** Because mainstream scholarship shares terminology with "civil war" and class analysis, the detector readily flags archaic White framing ("war of liberation") while catching Side B only on overt Marxist slurs ("class traitors," #10) or factual overreach (#8), likely missing subtler Red-POV framing that mimics neutral prose.
2. **No deduplication:** Counting near-identical revert-war retries separately turns one persistent Side A editor into multiple hits.
3. **Blind spot to false negatives:** Flagged positives alone prove nothing without auditing *unflagged* reverts to check Side B recall.

**Verdict:** skewed toward flagging A
